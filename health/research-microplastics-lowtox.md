# Research: Microplastics & Low-Tox Living (cited)

Deep-research pass, 2026-07-24. 30 sources fetched, 120 claims extracted, 25 adversarially verified (3-vote), 24 confirmed. Distinguishes real evidence from marketing.

## VERIFIED — high confidence

### Microplastics
- NEJM 2024 (Marfella et al.): carotid-plaque patients WITH micro/nanoplastics had 4.53x higher rate of MI/stroke/death (20% vs 7.5%). BUT observational ASSOCIATION, not causation; real limitations (possible surgical contamination, confounding). https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2309822
- Broader organ-harm claims = "suspected" from animal studies (rapid review: 28 animal / 3 human), not proven in humans. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11697325/
- Presence ≠ harmful dose: Taiwan fish risk assessment found hazard quotients <1.0, "no remarkable risk." https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8540681/

### The proven intervention = reduce intake (NOT a detox product)
- Cutting food-contact plastic (fresh food in GLASS, no canned/packaged) cut urinary BPA 59-66% + phthalates 37-56% in 3-7 days. Diet = primary exposure route. Rudel 2011 https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3223004/ ; PERTH Trial 2026 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04324-7
- CAVEAT: only works with COMPLETE swap to genuinely uncontaminated food — one study saw phthalates RISE when "fresh" food (dairy/coriander) was itself contaminated.
- NO commercial "microplastic detox" product has evidence — presume marketing/scam. Lever = reduce intake.

### Personal care
- Swapping to phthalate/paraben/triclosan/oxybenzone-free products cut those biomarkers 27-45% in 3 days (HERMOSA). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5047791/
- ANTI-MARKETING FLAG: some "free-of" products RAISED other chemicals (one paraben +101%) via substitutes. READ FULL INGREDIENT LISTS, don't trust front-label claims. (Use Yuka app / EWG Skin Deep.)
- Parabens: genuinely unsettled — FDA says no proven harm at cosmetic doses (butylparaben ~10,000-100,000x weaker than estradiol); endocrine reviews flag concern. Reduce, don't panic. https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/parabens-cosmetics
- Triclosan: strongest "avoid" case (thyroid/skin-barrier); FDA already banned it from OTC antibacterial soaps 2016.

### Fluoride vs nano-hydroxyapatite — RESOLVED 2026-07-30 (was OPEN)
- **NTP monograph (Aug 2024): moderate confidence that >1.5 mg/L in drinking water is associated with lower IQ in children.** US fluoridation = 0.7 mg/L, roughly half that; NTP said evidence was INSUFFICIENT to determine effects at US levels. https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/publications/monographs/mgraph08
- Sept 2024 Judge Chen ruled 0.7 mg/L an "unreasonable risk," ordered EPA action → **vacated by the 9th Circuit on procedural grounds** (post-2020 evidence improperly weighed), remanded. Genuinely unresolved. Coverage is dominated by advocacy sites both directions (fluoridealert.org anti, ilikemyteeth.org pro) — read accordingly.
- **The distinction that settles it: swallowed vs spat out.** Controversy = systemic lifelong ingestion. Toothpaste = topical, ~2 min, spit. Fluoride's anticavity mechanism is topical anyway. Only documented toothpaste risk is dental fluorosis, and only in kids under ~8 with still-forming enamel — not applicable to Gray at 24.
- **nHA is a legitimate swap, not a downgrade:** 18-month double-blind RCT, 189 adults, no new decay in 89.3% (nHA) vs 87.4% (fluoride), non-inferiority met. Replicated in children + high-caries orthodontic patients. Japanese standard since 1980. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10393266/ · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6590169/
- **Verdict for Gray: nHA (Davids Hydroxi, metal tube).** Meets his low-tox preference at no cavity-protection cost. Real exposure lever is NYC tap water (0.7 mg/L), not toothpaste.

### Miswak (Salvadora persica chewing stick) — verified 2026-07-30
- Meta-analyses: comparable to a standard toothbrush on plaque, possibly better on gingivitis. **Adjunctive use (alongside brushing) is where the effect is statistically significant** — as a replacement it's merely equivalent. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33513418/
- Limits: remineralization potential poor (inferior ion release); fibers run parallel to the handle so posterior/occlusal surfaces are hard to reach; 7 studies found higher gingival recession (10–68%), 2 found none — reviewers say evidence insufficient for causation, likely aggressive technique. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1319562X20301893
- Use as an add-on, light pressure, retrim the tip every 1–2 days, store dry. Only genuinely plastic-free tooth-cleaning option (all conventional bristles are nylon; bamboo brushes only change the handle).

## PRODUCT PICKS (2026-07-30)
Packaging now matters more than ingredients — microbeads were banned from US rinse-off products in 2015, so the remaining exposure is the tube/bottle shedding into contents.
- **Toothbrush:** keep the electric (gum-disease/glycemic-control link matters as a T1 diabetic). Miswak as a midday add-on.
- **Toothpaste:** Davids Hydroxi, nHA, metal tube.
- **Shampoo:** HiBAR — *syndet* bar (pH 4.5–5.5), not a soap bar. Most cheap "shampoo bars" are soap at pH 9–10 and wreck hair. No silicones/sulfates. Contains polyquaternium-7 = soluble, fine. Liquid alt: Plaine Products (refillable aluminum).
- **Soap:** any shea-heavy/superfatted cold-process bar in paper. Skip liquid body wash. Avoid drying bars — skin integrity matters for a diabetic.
- **Scan list (solid plastics, avoid):** polyethylene · polypropylene · nylon-6 · nylon-12 · PET · PMMA · acrylates copolymer · acrylates crosspolymer · styrene/acrylates copolymer (the shampoo "pearl" opacifier).
- **Ignore (soluble, not particulate):** PEG-## · carbomer · polyquaternium-## · glycerin.
